# In Vitro hERG Channel Inhibition by AXN-2401: A Cardiac Safety Signal

## Background

The human ether-a-go-go-related gene (hERG) potassium channel assay is a standard early screen for
cardiac liability, as hERG inhibition is mechanistically linked to QT interval prolongation and
risk of torsades de pointes. We report an independent academic evaluation of AXN-2401 in this
assay, undertaken as part of a broader survey of antifibrotic clinical candidates.

## Methods

AXN-2401 was tested in a heterologous hERG-expressing HEK293 cell line using automated patch-clamp
electrophysiology across a concentration range of 0.1 to 30 micromolar, run in our academic
electrophysiology core facility independent of the originating pharmaceutical sponsor.

## Results

AXN-2401 produced concentration-dependent inhibition of hERG tail current, with an IC50 of 4.8
micromolar in our assay conditions. Given reported total plasma exposure at clinically relevant oral
doses, this IC50 corresponds to a free-drug safety margin that we calculate as narrower than the
30-fold margin typically considered reassuring for early clinical development, raising a
potential cardiac safety signal that we believe warrants careful clinical ECG monitoring.

## Discussion

This in vitro signal does not by itself establish clinical risk of QT prolongation, since in vitro
hERG IC50 values can vary substantially between laboratories depending on assay conditions
(temperature, voltage protocol, seal quality), and free-drug exposure assumptions materially affect
the calculated safety margin. Nonetheless, we report this finding as a signal that should be
weighed against any internal or clinical cardiac safety data before drawing conclusions about the
cardiac safety of AXN-2401.
