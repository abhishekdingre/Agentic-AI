# The Changing Landscape of Antifibrotic Drug Targets: A Survey

## Introduction

Pulmonary fibrosis drug development has historically centered on a small number of approved
antifibrotic mechanisms with modest efficacy and significant tolerability challenges. This survey
reviews emerging target classes under investigation across the field, providing broader context
for newer mechanisms such as CRB3 antagonism.

## Established mechanisms

Approved antifibrotic therapies act through tyrosine kinase inhibition and modulation of
inflammatory and fibrotic cytokine pathways. Both classes slow, but do not halt or reverse,
progression of fibrosis, and are associated with gastrointestinal tolerability issues that limit
long-term adherence in some patients.

## Emerging mechanisms

Newer approaches under investigation include autotaxin inhibitors, integrin antagonists,
senolytic combinations targeting senescent fibrotic cells, and receptor-targeted antagonists aimed
at molecularly-defined patient subgroups rather than all-comers idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. The
subtype-stratified approach, in which patients are selected for trials based on a molecular
biomarker of pathway activity, is increasingly viewed as a way to improve the historically low
success rate of antifibrotic clinical trials by enriching for patients most likely to respond to a
given mechanism.

## Outlook

Targets with a plausible biological rationale, a tractable medicinal chemistry starting point, and
an associated patient-selection biomarker are viewed favorably by the field. Receptor antagonist
programs paired with a stratification biomarker, of the type described in recent CRB3/FBX9 axis
literature, are cited in this survey as an example of the subtype-stratified paradigm, though this
survey does not itself present new experimental data on any single compound or program.
