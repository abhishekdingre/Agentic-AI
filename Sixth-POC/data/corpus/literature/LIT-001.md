# CRB3 Signaling in Fibrotic Remodeling: A Review

## Abstract

Cribriform Repeat-Binding protein 3 (CRB3) is a cell-surface receptor expressed at low levels in
quiescent fibroblasts across most tissues. Over the past decade, a growing body of work has
implicated CRB3 as a central node in fibrotic tissue remodeling, particularly in the lung. This
review synthesizes the current understanding of CRB3 biology, its downstream signaling, and its
candidacy as a druggable target in fibrotic disease.

## CRB3 structure and expression

CRB3 is a type I transmembrane receptor with an extracellular ligand-binding domain and an
intracellular kinase-recruitment domain. In healthy adult lung tissue, CRB3 expression is largely
restricted to a small subpopulation of perivascular fibroblasts. Upon tissue injury, CRB3
expression is transcriptionally upregulated in activated myofibroblasts, where it becomes the
dominant driver of a downstream signaling cascade termed the CRB3/FBX9 axis.

## The CRB3/FBX9 axis

Ligand engagement of CRB3 triggers recruitment of the adaptor kinase FBX9, which in turn
phosphorylates downstream effectors controlling collagen type I and type III transcription. This
pathway is distinct from the canonical TGF-beta/SMAD axis and appears to operate in parallel,
amplifying collagen deposition independently of SMAD signaling. Because FBX9 is secreted in low
quantities into serum during active CRB3 signaling, circulating FBX9 has been proposed as a
pharmacodynamic biomarker of pathway activity.

## CRB3 as a druggable target

Several lines of evidence support CRB3 as a tractable small-molecule target: (1) the extracellular
ligand-binding domain has a well-defined hydrophobic pocket amenable to small-molecule antagonism;
(2) CRB3 knockout mice are viable and show no baseline pulmonary phenotype, suggesting the pathway
is dispensable in homeostasis and dispensable-pathway inhibition should carry a favorable safety
margin; (3) CRB3 upregulation correlates with disease severity across multiple fibrotic conditions
studied to date. Collectively, these observations position CRB3 as an attractive target for
antifibrotic drug development, motivating the medicinal chemistry campaigns described elsewhere in
this literature (see the AXN-2401 discovery report).
