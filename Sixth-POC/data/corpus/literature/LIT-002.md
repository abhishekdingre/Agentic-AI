# CRB3 Pathway Dysregulation Defines a Molecular Subtype of Pulmonary Fibrosis (PF-7)

## Background

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis is increasingly understood as a collection of molecularly distinct
subtypes rather than a single disease. This study set out to characterize a subtype, designated
PF-7, defined by elevated CRB3 pathway activity and distinguished from other subtypes by a
characteristic serum biomarker signature.

## Cohort and methods

We enrolled 214 patients with a clinical diagnosis of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and stratified
them using serum FBX9 concentration and lung biopsy CRB3 immunostaining intensity. Patients in the
top tertile of both measures were classified as PF-7. This subgroup comprised approximately 15% of
the overall cohort (32/214 patients).

## Findings

PF-7 patients showed a distinct radiographic progression pattern on serial CT compared to non-PF-7
patients, with faster decline in forced vital capacity (FVC) over 12 months (median decline 210 mL
vs 110 mL in non-PF-7 patients, p<0.01). Lung biopsy specimens from PF-7 patients showed
significantly denser CRB3-positive myofibroblast foci relative to non-PF-7 fibrotic tissue.
Elevated serum FBX9 correlated with biopsy CRB3 staining intensity (Spearman r=0.71), supporting
FBX9 as a usable non-invasive proxy for pathway activity in this subgroup.

## Interpretation

These data establish PF-7 as a molecularly and clinically distinguishable subtype of pulmonary
fibrosis in which the CRB3/FBX9 axis is disproportionately active, and in which faster disease
progression suggests an unmet need for a targeted therapy. The subtype definition and biomarker
panel used here (CRB3 IHC plus serum FBX9) are the basis for the diagnostic panel later
formalized for clinical trial enrollment (see patent filing on the CRB3/FBX9 biomarker panel).
This paper does not address renal fibrosis or any extrapulmonary fibrotic condition; the PF-7
classification described here is specific to the lung.
