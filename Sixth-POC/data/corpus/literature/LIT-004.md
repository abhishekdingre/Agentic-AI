# AXN-2401 Attenuates Bleomycin-Induced Lung Fibrosis in CRB3-Humanized Mice

## Study design

To test whether pharmacological CRB3 antagonism reduces fibrotic lung injury in vivo, we dosed
CRB3-humanized mice (in which the murine Crb3 extracellular domain is replaced with the human
sequence) with intratracheal bleomycin to induce lung fibrosis, then treated animals daily with
oral AXN-2401 (3, 10, or 30 mg/kg) or vehicle from day 7 to day 21 post-bleomycin.

## Efficacy endpoints

At day 21, AXN-2401 produced dose-dependent reductions in lung hydroxyproline content (a
quantitative measure of collagen deposition), with the 30 mg/kg dose reducing hydroxyproline
content by 58% relative to vehicle-treated bleomycin animals (p<0.001) and returning values close
to saline-only sham controls. Ashcroft histological fibrosis scores showed a corresponding
dose-dependent improvement. Serum FBX9, the pathway biomarker, decreased in a dose-dependent
manner that tracked with histological improvement, supporting FBX9 as a translatable
pharmacodynamic marker of target engagement across species.

## Pharmacokinetic-pharmacodynamic relationship

Plasma exposure at the 10 mg/kg dose was sufficient to maintain CRB3 target engagement (measured by
ex vivo receptor occupancy assay) above 80% for approximately 16 of the 24-hour dosing interval,
which corresponded to a partial efficacy response; the 30 mg/kg dose maintained greater than 80%
occupancy for the full interval and produced the maximal efficacy response observed in this study.

## Conclusion

These data provide the first in vivo pharmacological validation that CRB3 antagonism with AXN-2401
reduces fibrotic lung injury in a bleomycin model using human-sequence CRB3, supporting progression
of AXN-2401 into IND-enabling toxicology studies and, ultimately, clinical testing in PF-7 patients,
who share the CRB3-high molecular profile modeled by this humanized system.
