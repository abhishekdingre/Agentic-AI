# Discovery and Structure-Activity Relationships of AXN-2401, a Selective CRB3 Antagonist

## Introduction

Following identification of CRB3 as a candidate antifibrotic target, a medicinal chemistry
campaign was undertaken to identify a potent, selective, orally bioavailable small-molecule
antagonist of the CRB3 extracellular ligand-binding domain. This paper describes the discovery
route to the clinical candidate AXN-2401.

## Hit identification and optimization

Screening of a focused library against the CRB3 ligand-binding domain identified a pyrrolopyridine
hit series with micromolar affinity. Iterative structure-activity relationship (SAR) optimization,
guided by co-crystal structures of the series bound to the CRB3 extracellular domain, improved
binding affinity roughly 400-fold while maintaining aqueous solubility suitable for oral dosing.
The optimized compound, AXN-2401, is a pyrrolopyridine-carboxamide with a measured CRB3 binding
affinity (Kd) of 3.2 nM and greater than 1000-fold selectivity over a panel of 42 other
transmembrane receptors, including no appreciable binding to FBX9 itself.

## Cellular activity

In a cell-based reporter assay measuring CRB3-driven collagen promoter activity, AXN-2401 inhibited
ligand-induced signaling with an IC50 of 18 nM, consistent with its high-affinity binding to the
receptor. Selectivity profiling in a panel of 68 additional off-target receptors and enzymes
(CEREP-style panel) showed no significant activity (>50% inhibition) at 10 micromolar for any
target other than CRB3.

## Summary

AXN-2401 is a potent, selective, orally bioavailable small-molecule antagonist of CRB3, advanced
into preclinical efficacy testing (bleomycin mouse model) and subsequently into clinical
development. Full preclinical pharmacokinetic characterization is reported separately.
