# Internal Cardiac Safety Review: hERG and QTc Assessment for AXN-2401

## Purpose

This report was commissioned in direct response to a published independent academic study
reporting hERG channel inhibition by AXN-2401 (IC50 4.8 micromolar) and a resulting cardiac safety
signal. It compiles all internal data relevant to cardiac safety to assess whether that external
signal is corroborated internally.

## Internal in vitro hERG data

The internal safety pharmacology team independently tested AXN-2401 in a validated hERG-expressing
CHO cell line using manual patch-clamp electrophysiology, run under GLP conditions. The internal
assay measured a hERG IC50 of greater than 30 micromolar, substantially higher (i.e., less potent
hERG inhibition) than the 4.8 micromolar IC50 reported in the external academic publication. Using
the internally measured IC50 and clinical free-drug exposure at the 150 mg dose, the internally
calculated safety margin exceeds the standard 30-fold threshold considered reassuring, in contrast
to the narrower margin calculated in the external publication.

## Clinical corroboration

This internal in vitro finding is consistent with the absence of any QTc signal observed in the
dedicated Phase 2 cardiac safety substudy (continuous Holter ECG monitoring, 52 patients) and in
routine ECG monitoring across the Phase 1, Phase 2a, and open-label extension studies, none of
which detected clinically meaningful QTc prolongation at therapeutic doses.

## Assessment and open question

The internal safety pharmacology team's in vitro hERG result and the clinical ECG data are
internally consistent with each other but numerically disagree with the external academic
publication's in vitro IC50 value. Possible explanations considered include differences in cell
line, seal quality, or voltage protocol between the two assays, but this report does not claim to
have identified the specific source of the discrepancy. This report recommends continued ECG
monitoring in ongoing and future studies given that the external signal has not been fully
explained, and explicitly does not conclude that the external hERG finding is incorrect — only that
internal in vitro and clinical data have not corroborated a cardiac safety signal at therapeutic
exposures.
